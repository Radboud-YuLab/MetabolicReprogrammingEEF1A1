# MetabolicReprogrammingEEF1A1
Characterizing EEF1A1-dependent metabolic reprogramming through assessment of proteome reallocation
## Abstract
Metabolic reprogramming is the process in which cells adjust cellular metabolism to meet energetic and biosynthetic demands in response to changing cellular function or environmental conditions. Overflow metabolism is an example of metabolic reprograming in which, even in the presence of oxygen, glucose is not fully oxidized to CO2, but instead released as partially oxidized by-products such as lactate. Recent work has identified the translation elongation factor EEF1A1 as a regulator of overflow metabolism in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO)-K1 cells, with EEF1A1-deficient CHO-K1 cells (2E2 cells) exhibiting a strong preference for oxidative metabolic pathways. Here we further investigate the role of EEF1A1 in metabolic reprogramming, using a genome scale metabolic model, flux balance analyses, and proteomics. We find that EEF1A1-deficient CHO-K1 cells (2E2 cells) predominantly oxidize glucose, in contrast to wild-type (WT) cells that partially oxidize glucose and release carbon as lactate. Surprisingly, the preference for glucose oxidation in 2E2 cells was uncoupled from differences in growth rate and or glucose uptake. Genome-scale metabolic model (GEM) simulations show increased ATP synthesis rate, increased mitochondrial pyruvate transport, and increased flux through the TCA cycle in 2E2 cells. Additionally, GEM simulations reveal metabolic reprogramming in amino acid metabolism (AAM), where amino acid derivates are diverted into the mitochondria to increase acetyl-CoA and α-ketoglutarate flux. We find that 2E2 cells have reduced proteome allocation for glycolysis, but no change is observed for the TCA cycle or AAM pathways. This suggests that EEF1A1 not only regulates overflow metabolism through proteome reallocation but also through other mechanisms. This study further characterizes the role of EEF1A1 in regulating overflow metabolism, contributing to our understanding of the mechanistic link between metabolic reprogramming and proteome reallocation in mammalian cells.
